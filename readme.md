5. Important harvesting-tool recommendation

For your mixed seabed, I would prioritize a removable scoop with a retaining basket, rather than relying entirely on a conventional two-finger gripper.

Scoop and basket

Potentially gentler on sea cucumbers; useful where animals sit on sand or among small rocks.

Soft-jaw gripper

More precise for individual targets, but requires careful force control to avoid damaging sea cucumbers or sea urchins.

A basket should have openings small enough to retain the target species but large enough to let water and sediment pass through. Test it in a controlled area before collecting animals commercially.

#include <QObject>

#include <QProcess>

#include <QStringList>

class TrainingManager : public QObject

{

Q_OBJECT

public:

explicit TrainingManager(QObject *parent = nullptr)

: QObject(parent)

{

connect(&process, &QProcess::readyReadStandardOutput,

this, [this]() {

emit logMessage(

QString::fromLocal8Bit(

process.readAllStandardOutput()));

});

connect(&process, &QProcess::readyReadStandardError,

this, [this]() {

emit logMessage(

QString::fromLocal8Bit(

process.readAllStandardError()));

});

connect(&process,

QOverload<int, QProcess::ExitStatus>::of(

&QProcess::finished),

this, [this](int exitCode,

QProcess::ExitStatus status) {

emit trainingFinished(

status == QProcess::NormalExit && exitCode == 0,

exitCode);

});

}

**5. Is inductive touch better than normal physical buttons?**

Inductive buttons are better when environmental sealing is critical.

There are no moving button parts penetrating the front panel. The metal surface can be continuous, reducing the paths through which dust and water could enter. This can be particularly valuable for a portable instrument used outdoors or in wet environments.

Texas Instruments

Mechanical buttons are better for a low-cost first prototype.

Users can feel the click, accidental activation is easier to control through switch force and travel, and the wiring is simple. Replacing a failed button is also straightforward.

One important point: inductive touch buttons typically sense metal deformation or a change in the sensor's inductance. They are not necessarily activated by simply bringing a finger near the panel. A suitable design must account for panel thickness, press force, sensor geometry, electrical noise and button sensitivity.

My choice for your product:

Prototype: six sealed mechanical panel-mount buttons.

Production model requiring high water/dust resistance: evaluate a custom inductive keypad.

If the device will be cleaned frequently with liquids, specify and test the complete enclosure's IP rating rather than relying only on the button component's rating.
